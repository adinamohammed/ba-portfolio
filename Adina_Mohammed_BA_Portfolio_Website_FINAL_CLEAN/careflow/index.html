Section 01

Executive Summary

What CareFlow is, what it solves, and what it deliberately does not attempt.

Portfolio boundary: This is a self-initiated Business Analyst portfolio case study using fictional data for learning and demonstration purposes. It is not a live client project, production HMIS/EMR, or real hospital implementation.

CareFlow is a Smart OPD and Digital Patient Records System designed for a hypothetical multispeciality hospital scenario moving from manual outpatient workflows to a modern, structured digital coordination system.

In this hypothetical hospital scenario, the solution need was to register patients once and recognise them on every return visit, coordinate OPD flows without paper tokens, give doctors a clean workspace for consultation notes and orders, connect lab and pharmacy workflows, and give administrators real-time operational visibility.

CareFlow presents that target state through a self-initiated BA portfolio case study using an assumed solution-design model. The focus is requirements thinking, workflow design, validation, and stakeholder communication — not a live hospital deployment.

Section 02

Problem Statement

The hospital had no unified patient identity, no connected OPD workflow, and no real-time operational visibility.

🪪 REGISTRATION & IDENTITY
  • No permanent Patient ID — patients re-registered on every visit
  • No duplicate check — multiple files created for the same patient
  • Fragmented visit histories across paper files
🎟 OPD COORDINATION & TOKENS
  • Manual handwritten tokens with no queue visibility
  • No department-level queue data or wait time tracking
  • Urgent patients queued alongside routine visitors
📋 RECORDS & REPORTS
  • Patients carried their own paper files — frequently lost or incomplete
  • No process for tracking external lab reports
  • No distinction between hospital records and patient-provided documents
💊 PRESCRIPTIONS & PHARMACY
  • Handwritten prescriptions — misread drug names and incorrect quantities
  • No dispensing validation or partial dispense tracking
  • No confirmation that patients collected their medication
📊 ADMINISTRATIVE VISIBILITY
  • No real-time view of OPD footfall, queue depth, or resource utilisation
  • End-of-day manual counts from multiple paper sources
  • Reactive decision-making with no operational data
The hospital's outpatient department lacked a unified patient identity, structured visit coordination, connected clinical-to-pharmacy-to-lab workflows, and real-time administrative visibility — resulting in inefficiencies, data gaps, and a fragmented patient experience.

Doctor Adoption Concerns

Senior consultants were cautious about workflow disruption during busy OPD sessions. Addressed by keeping the consultation note simple (four sections), retaining a documented handwritten override, and framing the system as a structured record tool — not a gate on clinical judgment.

Reception Staff Concerns

Staff were concerned about accountability for data entry errors. Addressed by defining a clear amendment workflow with audit trail — errors are correctable with a reason recorded, not locked into a rigid system.

AS-IS vs TO-BE Patient Journey

From a paper-dependent manual OPD to a connected digital outpatient experience.

AS-IS — Current Journey
1
Patient arrives. Reception searches by name in register or basic system.
Risk of duplicate records — no reliable check
2
Handwritten or basic printed token issued. No queue visibility.
Patients crowd reception asking for updates
3
Patient waits — urgency handled informally by staff discretion.
No formal mechanism for urgent cases
4
Doctor reviews physical file. Patient recalls history verbally. Handwritten consultation note.
No access to previous reports if patient forgot them
5
Handwritten prescription given to patient. Handwritten lab request slip issued.
Misread drug names; lab slips lost
6
Patient pays at lab counter. Report printed and handed to patient.
Report lost before next visit
7
Pharmacist reads handwritten prescription manually. Dispenses by judgement.
No dispensing validation; partial dispense unrecorded
8
Admin compiles end-of-day counts manually from multiple paper sources.
Reactive decisions; no intraday visibility
TO-BE — CareFlow Journey
1
Patient arrives. QR scan or mobile lookup retrieves Patient ID in seconds. New patients registered with duplicate check.
Under 2 minutes for returning patients
2
Visit ID created. Digital token issued. Real-time queue display shows position.
Full transparency for patients and staff
3
Urgent patients flagged — moved to priority queue, payment block suspended automatically.
Clinical urgency always prioritised
4
Doctor sees structured visit record and all previous consultation summaries, prescriptions, and reports under the Patient ID.
Complete history at a glance
5
Doctor generates e-prescription and digital lab order. Referral initiated if needed — tracked in system.
Validated orders; referrals never silently abandoned
6
Lab report uploaded and linked to Visit ID. Available immediately in patient portal.
Reports never lost; clearly labelled by source
7
Pharmacist dispenses against system-validated e-prescription. Partial dispense recorded with reason.
Full dispensing audit trail
8
Admin views real-time OPD dashboard throughout the day.
Proactive decisions based on live data

Section 07

Core Modules

Eleven functional modules cover the full scope of CareFlow. Click any module to expand details.

M1 Hybrid Registration & Patient Lookup
ReceptionAdmin IT
▼
Create a single, reliable patient identity record that persists for life — eliminating duplicate registrations.
  • Walk-in and pre-registration with demographic data capture
  • Duplicate check using mobile number + date of birth before new ID created
  • Unique permanent Patient ID assigned on confirmed registration
  • QR code generated and linked to Patient ID
  • Returning patient lookup by QR scan, mobile number, or name+DOB
  • Data amendment with mandatory reason field and audit trail
  • Mobile number changes require identity verification; previous mobile number is retained in mobile change history for audit and lookup support
Scope boundary: Registration captures identity and contact information only. No clinical data collected at registration. Duplicate checks use matching combinations such as mobile number, name, and date of birth to flag possible existing records for staff review. The patient card contains Patient ID, name, DOB, and QR code — nothing clinical.
📦 Legacy Data Migration Approach
  • Existing hospital file numbers and card numbers mapped to new permanent Patient IDs
  • Basic patient master data (name, DOB, mobile, address) migrated first as the foundation layer
  • Important historical records scanned and attached in priority order (high-frequency patients first)
  • Patient-submitted old records labelled as Patient-Uploaded / Legacy Record — permanently labelled, never relabelled as hospital-generated
  • Duplicate records from old system flagged for staff review and manual resolution before migration is marked complete
M2 Digital & Physical Patient Card
ReceptionPatientsAll Staff (scan)
▼
A portable, scannable identity token connecting every patient to their permanent Patient ID across all future visits.
  • Physical card printed at first registration — name, Patient ID, QR code only
  • Replacement card printable on request (same Patient ID retained)
  • Digital card accessible through patient portal from day one
  • QR code scannable at any counter to retrieve Patient ID instantly
Card explicitly does NOT contain: Diagnosis, current complaint, department, blood group, medicines, test results, or any clinical data. A lost card carries zero clinical data risk. Blood group may be stored in the patient record for clinical use, but it is not displayed on the card. Patients can always be found by mobile number + name.
Final UI reference

The old static registration/visit form mockup has been removed. Use the Live OPD Prototype for the final OP Registration, patient lookup, visit creation, doctor selection, consultation fee status, and token generation flow.

M3 Visit Routing & Token Management
ReceptionPatientsDept. StaffAdmin
▼
Structured, visible, and fair queue management — with urgency prioritisation and real-time status for all parties.
Patient ID vs Visit ID: Patient ID P-10041 is permanent. Visit IDs V-20241101-001, V-20241115-002, and V-20241203-003 are created separately for each hospital visit.
  • New Visit ID created for every visit, linked to the permanent Patient ID
  • Department selected at token issuance — visit-specific, not based on prior visits
  • Token number displayed on waiting area screens with live queue position
  • Urgency flag by defined authorised roles — doctor, triage nurse, senior receptionist, or OPD supervisor — moves patient to priority queue and suspends payment block
  • Visit type recorded: New Visit or Follow-up
  • Token status: Waiting → Called → In Consultation → Completed
Scope boundary: OPD token routing only. Does not manage inpatient bed allocation, OT scheduling, or ED triage scoring.
M4 Doctor Consultation Module
OPD Doctors
▼
A clean, structured digital workspace for recording consultations, generating orders, and initiating referrals — linked to the specific visit.
  • Doctor views patient's previous consultation summaries, prescriptions, and lab reports (read-only panel)
  • Structured note: Chief Complaint · Examination Findings · Assessment · Plan (as entered by the doctor)
  • E-prescription generation with drug name, dosage, frequency, duration, instructions
  • Lab order generation with test name and urgency flag; in-house/external choice is confirmed during the lab/payment step
  • Referral initiation — internal department or external facility
  • Follow-up date and instructions recordable in Plan section
  • Note becomes read-only after doctor marks visit Consultation Complete (amendments require audit trail)
Important: The system records the assessment and plan as entered by the doctor. CareFlow does not generate diagnoses, suggest prescriptions, or make clinical recommendations. No AI clinical features in scope.
Final UI reference

The final Doctor Workspace is shown in the live prototype. It starts with doctor-side patient queue/search, then opens the selected patient record for vitals, complaint, assessment, lab order, e-prescription, and follow-up advice. Static older mockups were removed to keep one consistent UI story.

M5 Referral & Clearance Tracking
DoctorsDept. StaffAdmin
▼
Track referrals from creation to clearance — ensuring none are silently abandoned.
  • Internal referral: doctor selects target department, referral reason recorded, sent to target queue
  • Receiving department issues a referral token against the same Visit ID and ReferralID, so the patient is physically routed without creating a new Patient ID
  • External referral: receiving facility recorded, referral letter generated for print or portal
  • Status values: Pending → Accepted → Completed / Cancelled
  • Referrals open beyond 48 hours flagged on admin dashboard
  • Visit closure prompts confirmation if open referrals remain
Scope boundary: Coordination tracking only. No system-to-system integration with external hospitals in Phase 1.
M6 Lab Order, Payment Choice & Report Upload
DoctorsLab StaffReceptionPatients
▼
Connect lab orders to visit records, enforce payment integrity, and ensure every report — regardless of source — is permanently linked and labelled.
  • Lab order created in consultation, received immediately in lab module
  • Patient offered choice: in-house processing or external lab
  • In-house: payment collected before processing (urgency flag suspends this — sample taken, payment resolved post-sample)
  • In-house results uploaded by lab staff and auto-linked to Visit ID, labelled In-House
  • External reports uploaded by staff or patient — permanently labelled External Report
  • External lab status changes from External Pending to External Report Received after upload, so it no longer remains pending
  • Patient-uploaded historical records labelled Patient-Uploaded — immutable label with review status: Pending Review / Reviewed / Rejected
Scope boundary: Outpatient lab orders from visits only. Inpatient pathology, radiology scheduling, and report interpretation are out of scope.

Patient Access — Digital vs Printed Summaries

Digital visit summary is available by default in the patient portal immediately after visit closure — no staff action required.
Printed summary is request-based only, to reduce routine staff printing workload. Patients request via portal or reception; collected at the counter.

M7 E-Prescription & Pharmacy Dispensing
DoctorsPharmacists
▼
Replace handwritten prescriptions with a validated digital process that prevents dispensing errors and creates a complete dispensing record.
  • E-prescription generated by doctor, immediately visible in pharmacy module
  • Pharmacist verifies patient identity before dispensing
  • System prevents dispensing without a valid e-prescription linked to an active Visit ID
  • Partial dispensing supported — items not dispensed and reason recorded
  • Handwritten override permitted only with senior pharmacist authorisation + reason logging
Scope boundary: Outpatient dispensing of doctor-prescribed medications only. Inpatient IV pharmacy, controlled-substance specialised workflows, and full inpatient medication administration are out of scope for this project.
M8 Patient Digital Record
DoctorsReception (identity)IT Admin (audit)Patients (portal)
▼
A permanent, longitudinal record per Patient ID — growing with every visit, accessible to authorised users, never deleted.
  • One permanent record per Patient ID, created at first registration
  • Every visit creates a linked visit record with consultation summary, prescription, lab orders/reports, and referrals
  • Basic IP discharge summary available only for OP follow-up continuity reference (not full inpatient record)
  • IP discharge summary is attached by authorised clinical admin/medical records staff after discharge and is visible to OP doctors during follow-up
  • Records are never deleted — archived through a defined, authorised process
  • Every view and edit logged in the audit trail
Scope boundary: A structured record of OPD visits and their outputs. Not a full EMR or EHR. Nursing notes, ICU charts, and inpatient administration records are not stored here.
M9 Patient Portal & Record Access
Patients
▼
Self-service access to own health records — removing dependence on paper and enabling engagement with visit history at any time.
  • Login via mobile OTP — no separate password required
  • View full visit history: dates, departments, consultation summaries, prescriptions, reports
  • Download individual reports and prescription records
  • Upload external or old records — permanently labelled Patient-Uploaded
  • Uploaded records show verification status: Pending Review / Reviewed / Rejected; they do not become verified hospital records automatically
  • View referral status for active referrals
  • Digital visit summary available immediately on visit closure (default)
  • Request printed summary through portal — collected at reception
Phase 1 portal is read-and-upload only. Appointment booking, doctor messaging, and teleconsultation are Phase 3. Patient can view but cannot edit hospital-generated clinical records.
M10 Admin Dashboard
Hospital AdminOPD HeadDept. Heads
▼
Real-time, role-appropriate view of OPD operations — without exposing patient-level clinical data.
  • Real-time: registrations today, active tokens per dept., average wait time, pending lab payments, pharmacy status, open referrals
  • Daily panels: 7-day footfall trend, top departments by volume, lab turnaround, pharmacy completion rate
  • Filters: date range, department, doctor, visit type
  • CSV and PDF export of any panel (export logged in audit trail)
  • Role-specific views: Dept. Head sees own dept. only; OPD Head sees all; Admin sees full operational view
  • Drill-down to operational visit detail — stops at Visit ID level, never exposes clinical note content
Scope boundary: Aggregate operational data only. No patient-identifiable clinical records, consultation notes, or prescription details visible at dashboard level.
M11 Privacy, Consent & Role-Based Access
All UsersIT Admin (manages)Patients (consent held by)
▼
Every data interaction is authorised, audited, and aligned with the patient's expressed consent.
  • Treatment record consent is mandatory for creating and maintaining consultation, prescription, lab, billing, and follow-up records
  • Research / sharing consent is optional and opt-in only; patient can refuse it and still receive treatment
  • Consent revocation restricts future non-treatment sharing, while existing treatment records are retained for clinical and legal continuity
  • Role-based access is enforced at system level — users see only what their role needs
  • Emergency access is a controlled exception: senior clinician only, reason required, time-limited, logged, and reviewable later
  • Every record view, edit, download, and export is logged with User ID, timestamp, action type, and record reference
  • Audit logs are write-once and read-only — they cannot be edited or deleted by any role
Data retention: Records never deleted automatically. Archival is a managed, authorised process. Active records retained per hospital policy.
Section 08

Key Process Flows

Four core OPD swim-lane flows. Each actor's steps are shown in their lane with clear directional progression.

FLOW 1 — First-Time Patient Registration & OPD Routing
Patient
1. Arrives at OPD Reception → Provides name, mobile & DOB → Receives Patient Card & Token → Waits — watches queue display → Enters consultation room when called
Reception Staff
2. Runs duplicate check (mobile + DOB) → Match found? → No match: captures full biographic data → Selects department, sets Visit Type: New → Prints physical card & issues token
System
3. Duplicate check returns result → Assigns permanent Patient ID → Generates QR code → Creates Visit ID + Token Number → Token appears on queue display (<30s)
Urgency Branch
⚡ Authorised staff raises urgency flag → enters reason → patient moves to priority queue → Payment block suspended. Urgency reason recorded.
If a duplicate record is found during the check, reception reviews the match and either confirms the existing record or proceeds with new registration only after dismissing the alert. A new Patient ID is never created for a confirmed existing patient.
FLOW 2 — Returning Patient Lookup & OPD Routing
Patient
1. Arrives — presents patient card (or states mobile number) → Confirms identity verbally → Receives new token for today's visit → Waits — watches queue display → Enters consultation room when called
Reception Staff
2. Scans QR code — or searches by mobile + name → Confirms patient identity → Selects department for THIS visit (any dept.) → Sets Visit Type: Follow-up or New Complaint → Token issued — under 2 minutes total
System
3. Retrieves Patient ID (<3s) → No new Patient ID created — existing record used → Creates new Visit ID for today → New token linked to new Visit ID → Token on dept. queue display (<30s)
Key design principle: the Patient ID is permanent and never changes. Each visit creates a fresh Visit ID. Department routing is always chosen for the current visit — a patient's previous department does not restrict or pre-select today's routing.
FLOW 3 — Doctor Consultation, E-Prescription & Referral
Doctor
1. Opens active visit queue — selects patient's token → Reviews previous visit summaries (read-only panel) → Records consultation note (4 sections) → Generates e-prescription (if needed) → Raises lab order (if needed) → Initiates referral (if needed) → Marks visit: Consultation Complete
System
2. Loads visit record + patient history → Note timestamped + linked to Visit ID on save → E-prescription visible in pharmacy (<60s) → Lab order visible in lab module (<60s) → Referral sent to target dept. queue → Note locked (read-only after completion)
Pharmacy
3. Receives e-prescription in module → Verifies patient identity → Dispenses or records partial dispense
Referral Dept.
Internal referral received → Status: Pending → Receiving dept. issues referral token using same Visit ID + ReferralID → Dept. marks: Accepted → sees patient → Completed → ⚡ If open >48h → flagged on admin dashboard
The consultation note records the Assessment and Plan exactly as entered by the doctor. CareFlow does not generate or suggest clinical content — it is a structured record tool, not a clinical decision support system.
FLOW 4 — Lab Order, Payment Choice & Report Upload
Doctor
1. Raises lab order in consultation module → Specifies: test name and urgency flag (Y/N). Patient confirms in-house or external option at lab/payment step
System
2. Lab order appears in lab module (<60s) → Displays: patient name, Visit ID, test, urgency, lab type
Patient / Reception
3. In-house or External? → In-house: payment collected → Receipt linked to order → ⚡ Urgent flag: sample taken first → payment resolved post-sample
Lab Staff
4a. IN-HOUSE: Order moves to In Progress after payment → Sample processed → Result uploaded → auto-linked to Visit ID → labelled: In-House
External / Patient Upload
4b. EXTERNAL: Patient goes to external lab → Staff uploads report on patient's behalf — OR patient uploads via portal → Label: External Report (immutable — cannot be relabelled) → Historical uploads: Patient-Uploaded / Legacy Record (immutable)
Source labels are immutable. An External Report or Patient-Uploaded record can never be relabelled as a hospital-generated record. Doctors can always assess the provenance of any report they review.

Functional Requirements

Requirements in FR-ID format with acceptance criteria. Grouped by module. NFRs at the end.

📱
FR-M9 Portal Access Rule

Digital visit summary is available by default in the patient portal immediately after visit closure — no staff action required. Printed summary is request-based only, reducing routine staff printing workload.

M1 — Hybrid Registration & Patient Lookup
FR-M1-01
Duplicate check: The system shall perform a duplicate check using mobile number and date of birth before creating a new patient record. Must Have
Acceptance: Warning and existing match displayed if duplicate detected; new record cannot be created until check is reviewed and dismissed by authorised user.
FR-M1-02
Patient ID assignment: The system shall assign a unique, permanent Patient ID to every new patient at confirmed registration. Must Have
Acceptance: Patient ID generated within 3 seconds of record save; no two patients share an ID; ID never changes or is reassigned.
FR-M1-03
Returning patient lookup: The system shall support lookup by QR scan, mobile number, or name + date of birth. Must Have
Acceptance: Match returned within 3 seconds; multiple partial matches shown as a list for staff selection.
FR-M1-04
Data amendment: The system shall support amendments to existing patient records with a mandatory reason field and full audit trail. Must Have
Acceptance: Every amendment logs user ID, timestamp, original value, new value, and reason. Amended records remain fully functional.
FR-M1-05
Pre-registration: The system shall support optional pre-registration that is finalised at the reception counter on first physical arrival. Should Have
Acceptance: Pre-registration data held in pending state; Patient ID not assigned until counter confirmation.
M3 — Visit Routing & Token Management
FR-M3-01
Visit ID: The system shall create a unique Visit ID for every visit, linked to the patient's permanent Patient ID. Must Have
Acceptance: Visit ID format distinct from Patient ID; one per visit; never reused.
FR-M3-02
Department selection: The system shall allow any registered department to be selected at token issuance, independent of previous visit routing. Must Have
Acceptance: No restriction based on prior department history.
FR-M3-03
Queue display: The system shall display real-time token queue status on waiting area screens. Must Have
Acceptance: Queue display refreshes within 30 seconds of any token status change.
FR-M3-04
Urgency flag: The system shall support an urgency flag that moves the patient to priority queue and overrides any payment prerequisite. Must Have
Acceptance: Requires authorised user role and mandatory reason; flagged patient placed at top of relevant department queue immediately.
M4 — Doctor Consultation Module
FR-M4-01
Consultation note structure: The system shall present a structured note form with four sections: Chief Complaint, Examination Findings, Assessment, and Plan. Must Have
Acceptance: All four sections presented; Chief Complaint mandatory; note timestamped and linked to Visit ID on save. System does not generate or pre-fill clinical content.
FR-M4-02
Previous visit history: The system shall display the patient's previous visit summaries, prescriptions, and lab reports in a read-only panel during consultation. Must Have
Acceptance: All visits under same Patient ID visible; read-only; no edit capability from this panel.
FR-M4-03
E-prescription: The system shall allow the doctor to generate an e-prescription linked to the Visit ID, with drug name, dosage, frequency, duration, and instructions. Must Have
Acceptance: Prescription visible in pharmacy module within 60 seconds of generation.
FR-M4-04
Lab order: The system shall allow the doctor to raise a lab order with test name and urgency flag; in-house or external lab choice is confirmed during the lab/payment step. Must Have
Acceptance: Lab order visible in lab module within 60 seconds.
FR-M4-05
Note lock: The system shall mark the consultation note read-only after the doctor marks Consultation Complete, except for authorised amendments with audit trail. Must Have
Acceptance: Post-completion edits require senior authorisation and mandatory amendment reason; original note retained and amendment appended.
M5 — Referral & Clearance Tracking
FR-M5-01

Section 10

Business Rules

Rules governing system behaviour. Rules marked with a shield icon are patient-safety-critical.

BR-01
One permanent Patient ID is assigned per patient. A new Patient ID may not be created for a patient who already has a record in the system.
Prevents duplicate records and ensures all visit history is consolidated under a single identity.
Registration
BR-02
Before creating a new patient record, the system must check for an existing record using mobile number and date of birth. A new record may only be created if no confirmed match is found, or the match is reviewed and dismissed by an authorised user.
Duplicate check is the primary control against fragmented patient histories.
Registration
BR-03
Every visit must have its own unique Visit ID. A Patient ID must never be used as a Visit ID. Visit IDs are not recycled or reused.
Separates permanent patient identity from episodic visit activity, enabling clean longitudinal records.
Visit Management
BR-04
Department assignment is per visit. A patient's department for one visit does not restrict or pre-select the department for any future visit.
Patients at a multispeciality hospital visit different departments across different visits; routing must always reflect the current need.
Visit Management
BR-05 🛡
In-house lab payment must be confirmed before the lab order moves to In Progress. This rule may only be overridden when an authorised user has raised an urgency flag. The override reason must be recorded.
Ensures payment integrity while guaranteeing that clinical urgency is never blocked by administrative processes.
Lab
BR-06 🛡
Emergency and urgent visits must not be blocked at any stage — registration, token queue, lab, or pharmacy — due to pending payment or incomplete administrative steps. Urgency override is available at each stage and requires a reason to be recorded.
Patient safety is the overriding priority. Financial resolution always follows clinical resolution.
All Modules
BR-07 🛡
No pharmacy dispensing may occur without a valid e-prescription linked to an active Visit ID. The system must prevent dispensing if this condition is not met. A handwritten prescription exception is permitted only with senior pharmacist authorisation and mandatory reason logging.
Eliminates unvalidated dispensing, reduces medication errors, and creates a complete dispensing audit trail.
Pharmacy
BR-08
External lab reports and patient-uploaded records must be permanently labelled with their source at the time of upload. Source labels cannot be changed after upload. External reports cannot appear as hospital-generated records under any circumstances.
Maintains clinical record integrity. Doctors must know whether a report was generated under hospital quality controls or obtained externally.
Lab / Patient Record
BR-08A
Patient-uploaded records are treated as supporting documents only until reviewed by authorised clinical staff. They may be marked Pending Review, Reviewed, or Rejected, but their Patient-Uploaded source label remains unchanged.
Prevents forged, duplicate, unclear, or outdated documents from being mistaken as verified hospital-created records.
Patient Portal / Record Governance
BR-09
The patient card — physical or digital — must contain only: Patient ID, name, date of birth, and QR code. Clinical data including assessment and plan, medicines, test results, complaint, or department must never appear on the patient card.
Data minimisation principle. The card is an identity token, not a clinical summary. Loss of the card must carry zero clinical data risk.
Patient Card
BR-10
A printed visit summary is generated only on explicit patient or staff request. A digital visit summary is made available in the patient portal by default when the visit is closed.
Reduces unnecessary paper output while ensuring every patient has guaranteed digital access to their visit record without staff intervention.
Patient Record / Portal
BR-11
Referrals remain open until explicitly marked as Completed or Cancelled by an authorised user. A visit may be closed with open referrals only if the doctor explicitly acknowledges them at the time of closure.
Prevents referrals from being silently abandoned. Acknowledgement creates a documented record that the doctor was aware of the open referral.
Referral
BR-12
Role-based access is enforced at the system level. No user may access data categories outside their assigned role. Emergency access beyond role boundaries requires mandatory reason logging, senior-level authorisation, and time-limited access.
Protects patient data and limits the risk of accidental or deliberate unauthorised access.
Access Control
BR-13
A consent record must exist before any data sharing for non-treatment purposes. Research, analytics beyond operational reporting, or third-party sharing each require a specific, separately recorded consent.
Separates treatment consent from secondary use consent. Patients have a clear right to restrict non-treatment use of their data.
Consent
BR-14
Patient records are never deleted. Records no longer in active use are archived through a defined and authorised archival process. Archived records remain retrievable by IT Admin.
Healthcare records carry long-term clinical and legal significance. Deletion must not occur through routine system operation.
Patient Record
BR-15
All audit log entries are written once and are read-only. Audit logs cannot be edited, amended, or deleted by any user role — including IT Admin.
An editable audit log provides no assurance. Immutability is the minimum standard for a trustworthy audit trail.
Audit
BR-16
If a returning patient changes their registered mobile number, staff must verify identity using Patient ID/card, old mobile number if available, date of birth, or alternate contact before updating it. Previous mobile number is retained in mobile change history and the action is audit logged.
Prevents account takeover and preserves lookup continuity when mobile numbers change.
Registration / Audit
BR-17
Internal referrals use the same Visit ID and create a ReferralID-linked token for the receiving department. A referral must not create a new Patient ID or fragment the current visit history.
Keeps the patient physically routable between departments while preserving one connected visit trail.
Referral / Token
BR-18 🛡
The urgency flag may be set only by authorised roles: OPD Doctor, Triage Nurse, Senior Receptionist, or OPD Supervisor. The flag must include a mandatory reason before it can be saved.
Prevents casual queue/payment bypass while allowing urgent patient care to move faster when clinically or operationally justified.
Urgency / Token / Lab

Section 13

Privacy, Consent & Access Control

Every data interaction in CareFlow is authorised, audited, and aligned with the patient's expressed consent.

🗜
Data Minimisation

Only data necessary for the defined purpose is collected. The patient card contains only identity data. Aggregate dashboards contain no identifiable clinical content.

🎯
Purpose Limitation

Treatment data is used for treatment and direct hospital operations only. Research, marketing, advanced analytics beyond routine operations, and third-party sharing require separate, explicit, independently revocable consent.

✅
Consent-First

No patient data is shared for any non-treatment purpose without a recorded consent. Consent is revocable at any time. Revocation restricts future use — it does not erase past treatment records.

CONSENT EXAMPLE — TREATMENT VS OPTIONAL SHARING

Mandatory treatment consent: patient allows the hospital to create and maintain digital records for consultation, prescriptions, lab reports, billing, and follow-up care.

Optional research/sharing consent: patient may allow anonymised data to be used for research or allow records to be shared with an external specialist/partner hospital. Patient can refuse optional consent and still receive treatment.

AUDIT TRAIL — WHAT IS LOGGED
  • Every record view, edit, upload, and download
  • Every login, logout, and session timeout
  • Every report export — user ID, timestamp, panel
  • Every emergency access invocation — reason mandatory
  • Every consent update — before and after values
  • Every role change — authorising user logged
Immutability: Audit logs are write-once. No user — including IT Admin — can edit or delete them.
EMERGENCY ACCESS RULE — REASON + LIMITED TIME + LOG + REVIEW
  • Senior clinician may access records beyond normal role in a documented emergency
  • Mandatory reason required before access is granted
  • Access is time-limited — expires automatically
  • Event immediately flagged in audit log
  • Notification sent to IT Admin and Hospital Management
  • Use reviewed as part of regular audit activities

BA Reflection

Professional reflections on what worked, what was difficult, and what this engagement taught me.

✅
What Went Well
  • Early scope boundary statement (what CareFlow is NOT) was referenced repeatedly throughout — prevented scope creep in workshops and assumed implementation reviews
  • MoSCoW phasing exercise helped manage doctor adoption concerns; showing what was NOT changing on day one built clinical staff confidence
  • Immutable source label rule for external reports was agreed in a single lab module workshop — prevented a governance gap that would have been costly to fix post-build
  • Requirements traceability from business need → FR → UAT scenario kept requirements traceable and reduced rework risk
⚡
What Was Challenging
  • Holding the scope boundary as stakeholders began to see what was possible — especially around expanding the patient digital record toward a full EMR
  • Doctor adoption concerns required more nuance than anticipated — the issue was professional autonomy, not technology resistance. Framing the note as a record tool (not a gate) was the key shift
  • The terms "visit," "episode," and "encounter" were used interchangeably by different stakeholders — caused document inconsistencies before the glossary was introduced
  • Defining the IP discharge summary continuity boundary without expanding into full inpatient management. Final rule: only the discharge summary is attached for OP follow-up reference; full IP records remain out of scope
🔄
What I'd Do Differently
  • Involve pharmacy team one workshop earlier — by the time the e-prescription design was presented for review, they had formed assumptions that conflicted with what was designed
  • Run a live pilot of the token queue display with reception staff before finalising UI requirements — the refresh speed feedback came during UAT when it should have come during requirements
  • Build the project glossary on day one of stakeholder interviews, not mid-project after conflicting terminology had already entered draft documents

Key BA Lessons

Scope boundaries are deliverables, not disclaimers
A clearly stated out-of-scope list is as important as the feature list. In healthcare settings, where every stakeholder has a legitimate reason to want more, it is the primary defence against gradual expansion.
Data decisions made early become structural
The immutable source label rule was a half-sentence in a workshop. If it had not been agreed then, it would have become a post-build change request — or a governance gap discovered after simulated sign-off.
Patient safety rules must be designed in, not bolted on
The rule that urgent patients are never blocked by payment sounds obvious in retrospect. Without it being explicit in the business rules register, a development team might have built a simpler payment gate and left the exception as a manual workaround.
