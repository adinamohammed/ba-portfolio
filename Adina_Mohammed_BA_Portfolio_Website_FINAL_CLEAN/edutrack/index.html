Section 03

What Was Going Wrong

Before EduTrack, the admissions team had no central system. Leads arrived from multiple platforms, counsellors worked from personal notes, and managers had no daily view of what was happening.

Problem 01

No Central Lead Tracking

Student enquiries came in from YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, Google Ads, the website, and referrals — but were logged in separate spreadsheets. There was no single place to see all leads, their stage, or who was handling them.

Problem 02

Inconsistent Counsellor Follow-Up

Counsellors had no reminders, no assigned leads, and no system to log call outcomes. Follow-ups depended on individual memory. Genuine leads — including students blocked by payment issues — were often forgotten.

Problem 03

No Daily Reporting

Managers had no automated report. Every evening, they had to chase each counsellor individually to find out how many leads were contacted, how many were interested, and how many had converted — taking around 3 hours of manual effort each day in the project assumption.

Two supporting problems made this worse. First, many enquiries from digital platforms like YouTube and Instagram were accidental — students who were not seriously interested — yet these were mixed in with genuine leads, wasting counsellor time. Second, some students were genuinely interested but could not proceed due to loan rejections or payment delays. These leads had no separate tracking and were often lost.

Section 06

How the CRM Is Structured

EduTrack is built across three focused modules — each one handling a distinct part of the admissions workflow.

Module 01 · Lead Management

Capturing and Organising Every Enquiry

  • Log leads from all platforms — YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, Google Ads, website, and referral
  • Tag each lead as Genuine, Accidental, or Not Relevant at entry
  • Track lead stage — New, Contacted, Interested, Application, Enrolled, or Dropped
  • Flag students blocked by payment or loan issues for separate follow-up
Module 02 · Follow-Up Tracking

Helping Counsellors Stay on Top of Their Leads

  • Assign leads to specific counsellors
  • Set follow-up due dates and send reminders
  • Log call outcomes after each contact attempt
  • Escalate non-responsive leads after 3 working days
  • Smart Priority Flags label each lead so counsellors know who to contact first
Module 03 · Reporting & Automation

Giving Managers Daily Visibility — Without Manual Chasing

  • Auto-generate a daily lead summary report at 6 PM
  • Show quality of leads by platform — which sources bring genuine leads and enrolled students
  • Include a count of payment-blocked leads for Finance Coordinator review
  • Include a Smart Priority Flags summary so managers can focus on problem cases
  • Report sent automatically — no manual compilation needed

What Are Smart Priority Flags?

Smart Priority Flags are rule-based priority labels created from CRM fields already captured in the system — lead quality, awareness status, payment readiness, loan status, and last follow-up date. They help counsellors and managers quickly see which leads need attention first. They do not make decisions automatically.

FlagWhat It Means
🟢High PriorityGenuine lead, interested or at application stage, high payment readiness
🟡Needs AttentionNo follow-up update for 3+ working days, or follow-up is overdue
🔴Payment BlockedStudent is interested and payment-ready, but loan is rejected, delayed, or a partner issue
⚪Low IntentAccidental or not-relevant enquiry, or student is not yet aware of the program — marked as Low Intent for lower follow-up priority

Section 10

Reporting Logic & SQL Snapshot

A small reporting snapshot was included to show how CRM data can answer manager questions around lead source quality, payment blockers, and overdue follow-ups.

Lead Source Quality
Shows which platforms generate genuine enquiries
SELECT lead_source,
       COUNT(*) AS total_leads,
       SUM(CASE WHEN lead_quality = 'Genuine'
           THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS genuine_leads
FROM leads
GROUP BY lead_source;
Payment-Blocked Leads
Helps manager review loan/payment issues
SELECT lead_id, student_name, program_interest,
       loan_status, assigned_counsellor
FROM leads
WHERE priority_flag = 'Payment Blocked'
ORDER BY last_updated_date;
Needs Attention Leads
Finds leads with no update for 3+ working days
SELECT lead_id, student_name, assigned_counsellor,
       last_follow_up_date
FROM leads
WHERE last_follow_up_date <= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL 3 DAY
AND lead_stage NOT IN ('Enrolled','Dropped');
Smart Priority Flag Logic Example
Shows how CRM business rules can be converted into SQL CASE logic
SELECT student_name,
       lead_quality,
       loan_status,
       last_follow_up_date,
       CASE
         WHEN loan_status IN ('Rejected','Delayed','Partner Issue') THEN 'Payment Blocked'
         WHEN last_follow_up_date <= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL 3 DAY THEN 'Needs Attention'
         WHEN lead_quality = 'Genuine'
              AND lead_stage IN ('Interested','Application')
              AND payment_readiness = 'High' THEN 'High Priority'
         ELSE 'Normal'
       END AS priority_flag
FROM leads;
Why this matters: These SQL examples show how business questions can be converted into CRM reports, dashboard logic, and UAT checks. They are simplified MySQL-style reporting queries for portfolio demonstration. They are included to show reporting understanding, not advanced coding.

Section 12

How the Daily Report Works

The daily report was the biggest time drain for the Admissions Manager. EduTrack replaces the manual evening chase with an automated report sent at 6 PM every working day.

Before EduTrack

  • Manager messages each counsellor individually at end of day
  • Counsellors compile notes from spreadsheets or memory
  • Information arrives at different times, in different formats
  • Manager spends around 3 hours gathering and summarising in the project assumption
  • Payment-blocked students and overdue follow-ups often missed entirely

After EduTrack

  • At 6 PM, system automatically pulls all lead data updated that day
  • Smart Priority Flags applied across all active leads
  • System compiles daily summary — new leads, follow-ups done, enrolments, payment-blocked count, and flag breakdown
  • Report sent directly to the Admissions Manager
  • Manager checks only problem cases in an illustrative 25–30 minutes

What I Learned

This project taught me how to take a real operational problem — scattered leads, no follow-up system, no reporting — and turn it into a structured set of requirements and workflows. Mapping the AS-IS process first helped me understand exactly where the breakdowns were happening before proposing any solution. Designing Smart Priority Flags also taught me how to turn business logic into simple, rule-based system behaviour — and how to explain a feature clearly without overstating what it does.

One Challenge I Navigated

Keeping the Smart Priority Flags feature simple and honest was the most important design decision in this project. It was easy to make it sound more advanced than it is. The right call was to describe it accurately — rule-based priority labels built from data already in the system. That framing made the feature clearer, more honest, and easier for stakeholders to understand.

One Thing I Would Do Differently

I would involve the Finance Coordinator earlier in the discovery phase. The payment-blocked lead problem became clearer later in the process, and earlier input from finance would have shaped the requirements around loan status tracking more precisely from the start.
