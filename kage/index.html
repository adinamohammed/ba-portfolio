01 · Context

Research Basis & Project Context

The project is not positioned as a real implementation. It is a domain-informed BA case study based on practical exposure to gym operations.

Research basis

Inspired by real gym operations

This case study is based on observed gym operations and common fitness studio workflow patterns. The business logic focuses on daily operational problems: memberships, PT sessions, trainer availability, payments, renewals, freelancer billing, equipment maintenance, and reports.

Portfolio boundary

Designed and documented, not implemented live

All names, metrics, screens and business data are fictional and created for portfolio demonstration. The goal is to show BA thinking: workflow analysis, requirements, business rules, data modelling, UAT planning and stakeholder-friendly prototype communication.

02 · Problem

Business Problem

KAGE needed operational visibility without adding more manual follow-up work for the owner, front desk team and trainers.

01
Scattered membership recordsMember plans, expiry dates and status were difficult to manage consistently across spreadsheets and manual notes.
02
PT package tracking gapsTrainer allocation, session count, completion status and package balance were not visible in one place.
03
Trainer availability not visibleDuring busy hours, admin had to ask trainers directly before assigning new PT enquiries or walk-in requests.
04
Renewals depended on memoryMembership renewals and inactive members could be missed when follow-ups depended on manual checking.
05
Freelancer revenue confusionFreelancer access fees and per-client facility fees had to be separated from the freelancer’s own training income.
06
Equipment repair history was informalRepeated repairs and replacement decisions were hard to justify without structured maintenance logs.
07
Delayed management reportingRevenue, renewals, PT workload, freelancer income and maintenance costs were not available as a live operational view.

PT Client Allocation & Trainer Availability Flow

This flow explains how admin can allocate a PT client to a suitable trainer and then schedule sessions based on trainer/client availability.

01New PT enquiry

Admin captures the client goal, preferred timing, package type and expected start date.

02Trainer load checked

Admin checks trainer availability, active PT clients, specialisation and current time slots.

03Trainer allocated

A suitable trainer is assigned to the client for continuity across the transformation journey.

04Sessions scheduled

Session timing is planned with admin, trainer and client based on availability.

05Progress tracked

Completed sessions reduce package balance and trainer notes support progress tracking.

PT Trainer Allocation Rules

RuleBusiness meaning
Trainer continuityThe same trainer should continue with the PT client wherever possible for one month or more, so progress, exercise planning and accountability stay consistent.
Availability checkBefore allocation, admin checks trainer availability, current PT load, preferred time slots, client goal and trainer specialisation.
Multiple PT clientsA trainer can manage multiple PT clients across different time slots, alternate-day schedules or different days of the week.
Conflict controlThe system should avoid double-booking the same trainer at the same time unless a shared/group PT session is intentionally planned.
Client/trainer timingAdmin can allocate the trainer, but the trainer and client can also agree on suitable session timings within the package period.
Balance deductionPT package balance reduces only after the session is completed, not when it is only booked or cancelled before start time.

Important BA decision

06 · Differentiators

Freelancer, Renewal & Maintenance Logic

These business rules make the ERP more realistic than a simple gym membership app.

Freelancer Billing

Monthly payable changes with active clients

Freelancer clients are registered as KAGE members and pay their fixed gym package fee to KAGE. Any private PT coaching fee collected by the freelancer is outside KAGE billing scope.

MonthOwn FeeActive ClientsPer-Client FeeMonthly Payable
Month 1₹5,0004₹3,000₹17,000
Month 2₹5,0003₹3,000₹14,000
Month 3₹5,0005₹3,000₹20,000

Billing recalculates monthly because freelancer client count can change.

Renewals

Expiry-driven retention queue

System checks membership expiry daily and moves members through clear follow-up states.

7 days before expiryStatus becomes Renewal Due and appears in follow-up queue.
Expiry date passedStatus becomes Expired and remains visible for front desk follow-up.
3+ days after expiryStatus becomes Overdue Renewal with admin escalation flag.
Payment receivedStatus becomes Renewed and member is removed from queue.

Reminder channel is configurable: SMS, WhatsApp, email or manual call follow-up. No vendor-specific integration is defined in MVP.

Equipment

Repair frequency to replacement review

Repair logs help the owner compare recurring repair cost and decide whether equipment should be monitored, serviced or reviewed for replacement.

Treadmill 023 repairs / 6 months → Review
Leg Press1 repair / 6 months → Monitor
Decision-support rule

3 or more repairs in a rolling 6-month window triggers Replacement Review. The system flags the issue; the owner/admin makes the final replacement decision.

07 · Requirements

Functional Requirements & Business Rules

A short, visible summary of key requirements. Full artefacts remain linked in the downloads section.

Key Functional Requirements

FR-01

Create unique member profile with plan, expiry date and status.

FR-02

Allocate PT client to a suitable trainer and schedule sessions based on trainer/client availability.

FR-03

Prevent conflicting trainer schedules unless a shared/group PT session is intentionally planned.

FR-04

Deduct PT package balance only after session completion.

FR-05

Calculate freelancer monthly payable using own monthly fee and active client access fee.

FR-06

Flag renewal due members before expiry and overdue renewals after grace period.

FR-07

Track equipment repairs, cost and replacement review status.

FR-08

Show revenue, renewals, PT usage and maintenance KPIs in reports.

FR-09

Generate invoice number and track invoice sharing status for recorded payments.

FR-10

Cancel PT sessions without reducing package balance when cancellation happens before session start.

Key Business Rules

BR-01

A duplicate member cannot be created with the same phone/email combination.

BR-02

Only completed PT sessions reduce package balance.

BR-03

Trainer commission is calculated only for completed eligible sessions.

BR-04

Trainers can view estimated commission after package completion; final payout approval remains with Admin/Finance.

BR-05

Freelancer-owned training charges are not KAGE revenue.

BR-06

If freelancer monthly payable is overdue by more than 5 working days, gate flag activates for admin review. The threshold is configurable by Admin.

BR-07

Renewal Due flag appears 7 days before membership expiry.

BR-08

Equipment with 3 or more repairs in a rolling 6-month window is flagged for replacement review.

BR-09

Every payment must capture amount, mode, date, collected-by user, invoice number and payment status.

BR-10

Cancelled PT sessions do not reduce package balance and must be logged with reason and timestamp.

Non-functional requirements

Simple MVP NFRs

NFR-01

System should support simultaneous use by Admin, Front Desk, Trainer and Finance roles during operating hours.

NFR-02

Member, payment, PT session and freelancer billing records should be retained for a minimum of 3 years based on studio policy.

NFR-03

Member lookup, PT booking and billing actions should respond within 3 seconds under normal load.

NFR-04

Access to full revenue reports, commission values and freelancer billing details should be restricted by role. Trainers see assigned sessions and approved commission status, while Admin/Finance access payout and revenue data.

12 · BA Reflection

What I Learned From This Project

A short reflection on the BA decisions behind the ERP scope, billing logic and validation approach.

Scope control

ERP scope should stay connected, not oversized

While building KAGE, I learned that an ERP case study becomes useful only when the modules connect to real operational decisions. I kept the MVP focused on memberships, PT sessions, billing, renewals, freelancer access, equipment and reports because these areas directly affect daily gym operations.

Decision made

Freelancer billing is access-fee based

One important decision was to keep freelancer billing monthly and access-fee based instead of session-based. The freelancer may earn private coaching income separately, but KAGE needs to track only its own facility access revenue: own fee plus active client access fee.

If this were real

Validation would come from users

If this were a live gym project, I would validate the assumptions with the owner, front desk staff, trainers and freelancers before finalising rules. I would especially confirm renewal thresholds, trainer allocation rules, gate-flag policy and equipment replacement review criteria.

Reflection summary

This project helped me practise how a Business Analyst converts messy operations into clear workflows, business rules, data relationships, UAT scenarios and a stakeholder-friendly prototype without over-claiming it as a production system.
